# Explain the central limit theorem

Chapter section 12.3. Exponential data (waiting times, say) are strongly skewed: most values are small and a few are huge. Yet the **average** of 50 of them behaves very regularly.

In `answer.md`, in plain words (about 250 to 350 words), explain:

1. what the **central limit theorem** promises about the sample mean of independent draws from the exponential distribution;
2. what it does **not** promise (what happens to the shape of the data themselves?);
3. why the spread of the sample mean is `sigma / sqrt(n)`, and what that means for how much data you need to halve the error.

## Example

A start: "If I draw `n` waiting times and average them, then repeat that many times, the averages themselves form a bell-shaped histogram, even though..."
