# Bayes or base rate?

Chapter section 10.5. A doctor says: "This test is **99% accurate**. You tested positive." For a disease that **1 in 1,000** people have, a person with a positive test is far less than 99% likely to be sick.

In `answer.md` explain, in about 200 to 250 words:

1. why the posterior is so low, with a count of people (use 100,000 people, a sensitivity of 99% and a false positive rate of 1%);
2. what the **base rate** (the prior) has to do with it;
3. what would have to change for a positive test to be convincing.

A start: "Out of 100,000 people, 100 are sick and 99,900 are healthy. The test finds 99 of the sick ones, but also..."
