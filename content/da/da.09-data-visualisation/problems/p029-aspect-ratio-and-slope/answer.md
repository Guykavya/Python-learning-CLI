## Model answer

The **data** do not change, and neither do the axis limits. What changes is the shape of the plot area, and with it the **angle** of the line, which is what the eye reads as "how fast". The wide, flat figure
(about 3 degrees) makes growth look calm; the tall, narrow one (about 22 degrees) makes the same growth look dramatic. The slope is a **drawing choice**, not a fact in the data.

A reader who glances at the flat chart concludes "not much happens"; one who glances at the tall chart concludes "it is taking off". Neither reader was told the rule.

To avoid pushing the reader, use the guideline **banking to 45 degrees**: choose the aspect ratio so that the important slopes sit near 45 degrees, where the eye judges changes best.
Do not squeeze a time series into a thin strip to look calm, or stretch it to look dramatic. State the scale (axis labels with units, and the numbers in the title).

When two charts will be compared, draw them with the **same shape and the same scale**, so that equal slopes mean equal changes.

- [ ] compare panels on the same shape and the same scale
