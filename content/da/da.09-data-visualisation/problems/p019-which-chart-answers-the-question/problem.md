# Which chart answers the question?

Chapter section 4.9. A chart is an answer to a question. The question decides the chart, not the other way round.

For each of the eight questions below, write in `answer.md`: the **chart you would draw first** and **one sentence of reason**. Name one chart you would **avoid** for at least three of them.

1. Which of our six stores earned the most revenue last year?
2. How did monthly revenue change over the last two years?
3. How are customers' visits per month spread: typical value, skew, outliers?
4. Does a customer's distance from the store go with how often they visit?
5. What share of each store's sales comes from espresso, tea, pastry and cold drinks?
6. How satisfied are customers in each store, on a 1 to 5 scale?
7. Which weekdays and hours are busiest (a number for every weekday-hour pair)?
8. Do loyalty members spend more per visit, and how sure are we?

Aim for about 200 to 300 words in total.

## Example

"0. How did a single store's sales change over a year? A **line chart** of monthly revenue: the x axis is ordered time and a line shows the direction. I would avoid
a bar chart for dense time series."
