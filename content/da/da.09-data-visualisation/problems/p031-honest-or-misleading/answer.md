## Model answer

1. **Truncated bar axis.** The axis starts at 2.0, so the second bar looks over three times taller (219%) for a real growth of 11%: a lie factor near 20. The reader believes the revenue has exploded.
   Fix: start the bars at 0 (the bar length is the data) and write "+11%" in the title.
2. **Dual axis.** Each axis has its own scale chosen by the author, so the crossing of the lines is an artefact. In reality Airport is higher than Harbour in every month. The reader believes
   the stores overtake each other. Fix: one axis (the unit is the same), or index both series to 100.
3. **Pie with nine similar slices.** Angles of nearly equal slices cannot be ranked, and rainbow colours have no order. The reader cannot say which store is largest. Fix: a sorted horizontal bar
   chart with the values written next to the bars, in one colour.
4. **3D bars.** The perspective changes the size of the bars and the front bars hide the back ones, so values cannot be read. The reader sees a decoration, not the numbers.
   Fix: a flat sorted bar chart.
5. **Cherry-picked window and a loud title.** Three months out of 24 hide the season and the long-term trend, and "exploded" is a claim the chart does not show. The reader believes growth is
   unprecedented. Fix: draw all 24 months, start at 0, and state the change in numbers ("Revenue grew 11% from 2023 to 2024").
