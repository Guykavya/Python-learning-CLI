## Model answer

**The numbers.** The ten incomes add to 819,000, so the **mean** is 81,900 rupees. Sorted, the 5th and 6th values are 24,000 and 25,000, so the **median** is 24,500 rupees. Nine of the ten households earn less than the mean.

**Which one for a *typical household*.** The **median**. It is the value in the middle: half the households earn less, half earn more. One very rich household (the *outlier*) can move the mean enormously, but it moves the median only by one position. Without the owner the mean would be about 24,333, almost the median: the one household accounts for nearly all of the difference.

**What the gap means.** A mean that sits far above the median is the signature of *skew* to the right: a long tail of large values. Incomes, house prices and response times are usually like this. The gap itself is information, and the rule of thumb of the chapter is "when they differ a lot, say so".

**When the mean is still right.** The mean is the *share* each household would get if the total were split equally: total income divided by households. For a question about a total (how much tax can the town collect? what is the payroll bill?) the mean is exactly the right number, because mean times count gives the total back. The median does not.

**What I would tell the council.** "The *median* household earns about 24,500 a month; the mean is 81,900 because one household earns 600,000. A support scheme should be planned around the median, and we should report both numbers with the reason they differ."
