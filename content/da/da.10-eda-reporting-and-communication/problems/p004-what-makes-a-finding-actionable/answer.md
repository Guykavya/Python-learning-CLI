## Model answer

A finding exists to **change a decision**. The reader is busy and may stop after two lines, so the first line must already contain the answer: what changed, by how much, and why. The path you took is how you *thought*; it belongs in an appendix for the one reader who wants to check it.

A recommendation without an **owner** is a wish: nobody is responsible, so nobody acts. Without a **date** there is no moment to check whether it happened. An owner and a date turn "we should look into delivery" into a task someone can be asked about.

The impact is a **range** in the business's own units (customers, rupees, hours) because the reader has to weigh it against the cost of acting, and because a single number pretends to a precision that data never have. "60 to 70 customers a month" can be compared with a budget; "an odds ratio of 2.9" cannot.

You state the **confidence** and the **limitations** yourself because a reasonable reader will think of them anyway. If they find the doubt first, they stop trusting the whole page; if you name it, they trust the rest and know what would change your mind. Confidence also tells them how big a bet the evidence supports.

Finally, a **success measure** ("churn back to 4 to 5 percent within three months") makes the finding testable after the decision.

The single test: **if this finding were accepted, who would do what differently, by when, and how would we know it worked?** If you cannot answer, it is not yet actionable.

**Checklist: your answer should mention**
- [ ] the decision the finding is meant to change
- [ ] answer first, because readers stop early
- [ ] who acts (owner) and by when (date)
- [ ] how big: a range in business units, against the cost
- [ ] how sure (confidence) and what could make it wrong (limitations)
- [ ] a success measure / the "who would do what differently" test
