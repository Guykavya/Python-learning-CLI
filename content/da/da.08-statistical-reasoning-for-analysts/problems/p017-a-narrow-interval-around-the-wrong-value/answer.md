## Model answer

I would trust the **random sample** of 400 customers: its interval, 2,420 to 2,580, is wide but honest, and it is built from a procedure
in which every customer had a chance to be chosen. The opt-in pop-up is answered by customers who choose to answer, probably the
more active and better-off ones, so its mean (2,950) is aimed at the wrong target.

A confidence interval measures **sampling error only**: how much the estimate would vary if the same procedure were repeated. It does
not measure **bias** from who was in the sample. The narrow interval 2,940 to 2,960 says that the opt-in procedure is very repeatable,
not that it is right.

Bias does not shrink with n. As more people opt in, the interval gets narrower and narrower around the biased value: the estimate
becomes **more confidently wrong**. That is why 200,000 self-selected answers can be worse than 400 random ones.

To improve the first source, fix the **sampling**, not the size: draw a random sample, follow up non-responders, or weight by known
population shares and say that weighting cannot correct for traits we did not measure. I would not simply collect more opt-in answers.
